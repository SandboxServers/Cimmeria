# LR-11 worknote: recorded window clicks become `@window_click`

> Type: worknote. Packet: [LR-11](../work-packets-2.md#lr-11-window-clicks-become-window_click).
> Decision: D-LR9 G1. Offline only: no lab, no running client.

## What was built

- **Sampler.** `ClientProbe::clicks()` returns the `ui.click` events the
  event store took in since the last call, through the store's named cursor
  `record` (`StoreInner::take_kind`), as `{ clicks, gap }`. The live probe
  (`supervisor/record_slot.rs`) reads only the store, so it checks the lease
  permit itself, as `camera_read` does. Each tick reads it right after the
  pump and fills `Tick.clicks`; `begin` pumps, then reads it once and
  discards the result, because clicks already in the store or still in the
  Lua ring came before the recording (the cursor outlives a recording, and
  is unset on an instance's first one). A `gap` (clicks evicted unread) is a
  `client:` tick error. The ring writes `mouse = ""` for a button it cannot
  name; the sampler reads that as no mouse button.
- **Log.** `UiClick` gained `mouse` (`left`, `right`, `middle` or the raw
  value), optional and left out when absent, so old logs still read.
- **Segment.** `RecordedRow.clicks` holds `(tick, UiClick)`: a click joins
  the row whose ticks hold it (the later row when two anchors share a tick);
  one before the first anchor goes nowhere, as a call does. A row anchored
  by `useItem`, `moveItem` or `useAbility` also takes the clicks of the tick
  before its anchor (`segment::click_ticks`): the click is in the store by a
  tick's pump while the call it sent can reach the tap after that tick's
  drain, so a hotbar or bag click can land a tick ahead of its anchor. In
  the previous row it would become a stray `@window_click` there; in its
  anchor's row the drop rule (3 below) sees it.
- **Emit** (`emit_clicks.rs`, a child of `emit_row.rs`). Per click, in this
  order:
  1. A click on the bare `BackgroundWin` (window and widget both
     `BackgroundWin`) is dropped: a world click, which the server sees as
     `interact` or `setTargetID`.
  2. Anything but a left click on a named widget is a TODO line
     `ui click <window>/<widget>: <why>`: a right, middle, unknown or missing
     mouse button; no widget name (empty or CEGUI's `__cewin_uid_` counter
     name); a control character in the name; no window; a click on the
     window itself; an `__auto_` part other than the close button.
  3. A click that replays through a step its call maps to is dropped: a
     click on `DialogWin` with a `dialogButtonChoice` in the click's tick or
     a neighbour; a click on any other window with the row's `useItem`,
     `moveItem` or `useAbility` anchor in the click's tick or a neighbour,
     except a title-bar close button (`__auto_closebutton__`): no item or
     ability call replays a close, so it stays a step.
  4. Otherwise, `@wait_for { lua_condition = "<window> ~= nil and
     <window>:isVisible()", timeout_ms }` (only when the window is a plain
     Lua identifier) and then `@window_click { target = "<widget>" }`,
     labelled `x` for a close button and `click` otherwise. The wait's
     timeout is D-LR7's dialog rule on the time from the anchor to the
     click's tick.
- **Window TODOs.** A window closing is explained by a click on that window
  in its tick, the tick before or the tick after (it was before or the same
  tick only), but only a click the row writes, as a step or a TODO: one
  dropped for its call does not replay the close, so the close stays a TODO.
  (A neighbour tick that is another row's counts its clicks as they are.) A
  window opening is never explained by a click: a click inside a window
  cannot have opened it, so an opening with no call is always a TODO, even
  with a click in that window right after it, which is still its own step.
- **Order in a tick:** clicks, then calls, then server events, then window
  TODOs (LR-05 had calls first).

The `@window_click` argument is `target`, as the spec said
(`WindowClickArgs.target`, `server/ui/args.rs`).

## Deviations from the packet

- **Neighbour ticks.** Each tick pumps (reads clicks) before it drains the
  tap and before it reads the UI. A click made between the pump and those
  reads shows its call or its window change in one tick and the click in
  the next. So "the same tick" is the click's tick or a neighbour, for both
  the drop rule and the window-TODO rule.
- **Which call is a click's.** The packet drops a click "whose window also
  produced a server call". A call names no window, so the pairing is by
  kind: dialog choices are `DialogWin`'s, item and ability anchors are any
  other window's; `interact` and region anchors are never a window's. A
  click on another window beside a dialog choice is kept (FS-P4's tutorial
  X right before the dialog's close is that case). Every dialog choice
  drops a `DialogWin` click, including a choice the mapping writes as a
  TODO (more than one button), so such a row has the choice TODO and no
  click step.
- **Clicks lead a tick.** A tick cannot order a click against a call in it,
  and a click usually causes one; with calls first, FS-P4's X and the
  dialog close in one tick would replay in the wrong order.
- **The wait before the click** is not in the packet; FS-P4's hand-written
  row has it (the tutorial can open seconds after the interact), and without
  it a replay clicks before the window exists.
- **A control character in a widget name** makes the click a TODO: the
  target would otherwise be the draft's only multi-line TOML string (the
  `toml` crate writes a string with a newline as `"""`). It still parses;
  the rule keeps every step on one line.
- **`BackgroundWin` drop** is an assumption about the client (that the
  world view is the full-screen background window); it is the one rule
  here not checked against a record.
